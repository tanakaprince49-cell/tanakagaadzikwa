# Press pitch kit — getting the 3 articles that unlock the panel

The goal is not "going viral". It is **three credible articles that spell the name
right and call you the founder of LINKUP**. That is the trigger.

---

## The story, in one line

> A 15-year-old in Harare built an AI app that matches founders with co-founders,
> and it is now pitching global investors.

Everything below is a variation on that.

---

## Angles (use the one that fits the outlet)

**1. The age story** — the strongest for general and international press.
A teenager building an AI startup from Harare, shipping to Google Play, while still
at school.

**2. The a16z story** — for tech/business outlets.
A 15-year-old from Zimbabwe is deliberately trying to get in front of Silicon Valley's
top investors. Provocative, quotable, and it is already the frame of your Wetindey Tech
cover.

**3. The build-in-public story** — for founder/startup press.
Documenting the whole journey on LinkedIn, crossing 2,000 then 3,000 followers,
showing revenue-less progress openly. Good for TechCabal / Disrupt Africa.

**4. The "solving a real problem" story** — for serious tech press.
Co-founder matching is a genuine unsolved problem in African tech specifically:
talent is everywhere, networks are not. LINKUP attacks distribution of networks
rather than distribution of capital.

> **Timely hook:** your LinkedIn post says LINKUP was **selected for the LvlUp
> Ventures Power of the Pitch Week, 7–11 December 2026**. Confirm the details before
> you pitch it, then lead with it — "selected to pitch to 200+ investors" is the
> single most newsworthy line you currently have, and it has a deadline attached.

## The email that works

Keep it under 150 words. Journalists do not read long pitches.

> **Subject:** 15-year-old in Harare built an AI co-founder matching app — pitching investors in December
>
> Hi [name],
>
> I'm Tanaka Gaadzikwa, 15, based in Harare. I built LINKUP, an AI app that matches
> founders with co-founders — it's live on Google Play and we've been selected to
> pitch at LvlUp Ventures' Power of the Pitch Week in December.
>
> I'm building this while still at school, and I think the story is less "teenager
> does tech" and more that the hardest problem for African founders isn't capital,
> it's finding each other.
>
> Happy to do an interview any time, or send more detail. Photos and a full fact
> sheet: tanakagaadzikwa.com/press
>
> Tanaka
> Founder & CEO, LINKUP
> tanakaprincede@… / +263 …

**Rules that matter:**

- Use the exact spelling `Tanaka Gaadzikwa` in the subject or first line.
- Always give the outlet an easy link — `/press` and `/about` exist for this.
- Never send a press release with no news hook on a Friday.
- If they interview you, ask them to link to `https://www.tanakagaadzikwa.com/`.
  That link is the whole point.

## After an article is published

1. Add it to `PERSON.sameAs` — no, not yet. First **share it** from LinkedIn and X
   in your own words; the backlink matters more than the share.
2. Once you have **two or three**, run
   [`wikidata-quickstatements.txt`](./wikidata-quickstatements.txt).
3. Reply to the journalist with a thank-you and one follow-up fact. That is how the
   second article happens.

---

## What not to do

- **Don't buy press.** Paid "as featured in" placements on sites Google treats as
  link farms do nothing for entity confidence and can hurt.
- **Don't create a Wikipedia article about yourself.** It will be deleted, and it
  makes the next attempt harder. Wikidata first, Wikipedia only if an independent
  editor decides you meet the notability bar.
- **Don't spam 200 outlets.** Three real relationships beat 200 blind emails.
- **Don't change the name anywhere.** Not in the bio, not in a headline, not on one
  outlet "for style". One consistent string is the entire game.
